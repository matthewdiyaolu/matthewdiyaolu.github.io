Research

Dissertation

Off the Books: How Child Marriage Law Pushes Young Couples into Religious Unions

ChapterWork in progress

In 2019 Indonesia raised the minimum legal age of marriage for women from 16 to 19. This paper studies whether the reform reduced early marriage or instead pushed young couples into unregistered religious unions that sit outside the law, using national household survey microdata.

A Year for the Nation or a Year Lost? National Service and Labor Market Outcomes in Nigeria

ChapterWork in progress

Nigerian university graduates must complete a year of national service before entering formal employment, and eligibility depends on age at graduation. I use this age cut-off to ask whether compulsory service eases the school-to-work transition or delays graduates’ entry into productive employment.

Equal Outcome, Unequal Investment: Observability and Household Spending on Fostered Children in Nigeria

ChapterDraft in preparation

Many Nigerian households raise fostered children alongside their own. This paper studies how the observability of household spending shapes investment in fostered children relative to biological children.

Do Moral Frames Reduce Congestion and Environmental Externalities? Evidence from an Incentivized Laboratory Experiment on Tourism Choices

Work in progress

An incentivized laboratory experiment testing whether moral framing changes the choices people make when their individual decisions add to congestion and environmental damage.

Do Child Protection Laws Change Fertility and Maternal Investment Decisions? Evidence from Nigeria’s Child Rights Act

Work in progress

Nigerian states adopted the Child Rights Act at different times. This paper uses that variation to study whether child protection laws change fertility decisions and mothers’ investment in their children.
